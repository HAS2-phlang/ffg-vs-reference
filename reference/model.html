How the money and the ownership actually work.

Foundry for Good is a private venture vehicle, and its economics are deliberately plain. Here is exactly how a company gets funded, who owns what, and what a founding team can expect from day one through profitability.

We fund the company: not just the idea, and not off your personal balance sheet.

Most people who want to start something never do, because starting means giving up a salary and self-funding through the most fragile stage of a business. The investment arm removes that barrier. Foundry for Good deploys permanent capital from its own balance sheet: no outside fund, no fund cycle. It capitalizes each venture directly, committing $250K per venture so the founding team can build full-time with a real runway behind them.

That capital does three things at once: it pays the founding team a base salary from day one, it funds the tools and hires needed to reach the first milestones, and it gives the business roughly two years of runway to find its footing before it needs to stand on its own revenue.

This is not a passive check. We co-found. From the first week, the founding team works alongside operators who have built in this exact market and draws on shared marketing, digital PR, finance and accounting, network, and recruiting functions instead of assembling them from scratch. The company is yours to run. The platform behind it is already built.

Capital structure

Who owns what, and why.

Each venture is its own company with its own cap table. The split is designed so the founding team owns a meaningful stake and the investment arm is compensated for the capital and platform it puts at risk.

Founding team

15–45% ownership

The people building the company hold a real equity stake: sized to the role, the contribution, and the stage they join. This is ownership, not options theater.

Foundry for Good

The studio stake

FFG holds the majority stake in exchange for the capital, the two-year runway, and full access to a fourteen-year operating platform: the assets that de-risk the build.

The company

Independent entity

Each venture is incorporated on its own, with its own P&L and leadership. It isn't a project inside FFG: it's a business the founding team runs.

How value comes back to founders

Because the capital is permanent, ventures are built to be profitable rather than to chase the next funding round, and ownership converts into real outcomes. As a company becomes cash-generative, its owners can take distributions from profit, position the business for a future sale, or keep compounding a healthy, independent company. The equity you hold is a claim on all three paths.

The exact terms of any individual venture are finalized in a founding agreement: this page describes the shape of the deal, not a substitute for it.

Stage 01

Vision

A defensible thesis and a named customer, validated before we choose your operator.

Every venture begins with a sharp, defensible point of view on an unserved corner of the social good economy, and we maintain a roster of validated venture candidates awaiting an operator. Before we search for that operator, the diligence is already done: customers identified, contacts, agreements, & strategies secured, and the core problems the venture will solve defined. The theses come from our 14 years in the industry and from direct conversations with leading clients. We then re-validate until the market, the buyer, and the path to first revenue are concrete. Only then do we define the ideal operator profile: the person we want at the table.

Stage 02

Incubate

Capital, a founding team, and 14 years of shared rails. Getting Attention was profitable at 18 months.

Once diligence is complete, we capitalize the venture and begin incorporation. We build the founding team from the strongest performers across the FFG ecosystem, then confirm the operator. The founding team draws a salary from day one and plugs into shared marketing, finance, recruiting, and go-to-market functions refined over 14 years. Nothing starts from scratch: each venture builds on what the last one proved. The goal of this stage is proof: (1) a working product in front of real customers, and (2) the first signs of repeatable demand. Getting Attention reached both and turned profitable eighteen months in, backed by the shared resources of 7 companies and a secured, vetted bench of specialized talent.

Stage 03

Operate

Your own company, run by you, backed by us with weekly mentorship.

Once the model is working, the venture graduates into a company of its own, with its own P&L, brand, leadership, and roadmap. From here you run the business; our value-creation specialists work with you weekly and bring support where you ask for it. Our record rests on being active, engaged investors who keep the operator independent. The company is yours to scale.

Stage 04

Compound

Profitable and independent: take distributions, sell, or keep compounding.

Because ventures are built to be profitable rather than to chase the next round, the venture lead holds real optionality. As the business generates cash, you can take distributions, position the company for a future sale, or keep compounding a healthy, independent business for years. Our capital is permanent: no fund clock forces an exit. Your equity is a claim on all three paths, and the longer the company compounds, the more that stake is worth.

Common questions

The things founders ask first.

No. Foundry for Good funds the company. You draw a base salary from day one and build full-time: you are not expected to self-fund the business or work for equity alone.

Founding teams hold 15–45% of the company they build. Where you land in that range depends on your role, the stage you join, and the scope of what you own operationally.

Each venture is funded to reach real milestones: $250K per venture, sized to give the company roughly two years of runway. The precise figure is set per venture.

Not every venture finds its market, and the model accounts for that. Because Foundry for Good provides the capital and runway, a founding team is not left personally in debt if a venture is wound down: and the operators who ran it well are people we want to build with again.

Often we do: years inside the sector surface problems worth solving, and we validate them before a founding team is brought on. We also partner with operators who arrive with their own sharp wedge into the social good economy.

We pair complementary co-founders: typically a business lead and a technical lead: who can commit full-time. See For Founders for exactly who thrives in the model and how to apply.
