Overview / About the Studio

The investment arm of Foundry for Good.

Foundry for Good is investing and building the best mission-driven companies in the U.S. The venture studio is the vehicle that does it: permanent capital, an active operating role, and a fourteen-year platform behind every company we back.

In one paragraph

We are the investors and operators behind seven profitable companies in the social good economy, and the studio is how we build the next seven.

Foundry for Good has spent fourteen years starting, acquiring, and scaling companies that serve nonprofits, associations, and the organizations working on behalf of them. Double the Donation, Nexus Marketing, Getting Attention, Tradewing, NXUnite, eCardWidget, and Fuller Focus were all built or bought inside that period, and all of them are operating today.

The venture studio is the investment arm of that group. It is a private venture vehicle: not a fund, not an accelerator, and not a passive minority investor. It capitalizes new companies directly from the balance sheet of the operating businesses, it staffs them from a 140-person bench, and it holds them.

Our approach is uniquely active. We provide capital and manpower, and we connect the companies we back to their target audience with the help of our industry experts. We believe social good isn't achieved by sitting down.

What the studio does

Four mandates, one balance sheet.

Every dollar the studio deploys does one of four jobs. The mandate determines the diligence, the ownership, and the people we put behind it.

Mandate 01

Build

We originate a company against a validated thesis, capitalize it, and pair it with a business lead and a technical lead. Getting Attention was built this way, with zero outside investment.

Mandate 02

Acquire

We buy businesses with existing infrastructure and proven leaders, then put the platform behind them. Tradewing was acquired in October 2024 and rebuilt from the brand down.

Mandate 03

Scale

We stay in the operating seat: bi-weekly reviews, shared engineering and go-to-market, and hiring support. The studio is measured on what its companies do after the money lands.

Mandate 04

Hold

Capital is permanent, so nothing is built to be flipped on a fund's schedule. Companies compound inside the portfolio until an exit is genuinely the right call for the owners.

How we differ

What this is, and what it is not.

Most people meeting the studio for the first time try to place it against a model they already know. Here is where it lands.

Not a venture fund
There are no limited partners, no committed fund size, and no ten-year clock. The capital comes from the retained profits of the operating companies, which means we answer to the businesses, not to a fundraising calendar.
Not an accelerator
There is no batch, no demo day, and no standard cheque in exchange for a small stake. We take a majority position and put a founding team, a salary, and roughly two years of runway behind each company.
Not a passive investor
We co-found. Founding teams work alongside operators who have built in this exact market, and draw on shared engineering, growth, and finance functions rather than assembling them from scratch.
Not a services agency
Each venture is incorporated on its own, with its own P&L, brand, leadership, and roadmap. It is a business the founding team runs, not a project inside Foundry for Good.
What it is
A private venture vehicle with permanent capital, operating only in the social good economy, run by the people who built the seven companies already in the portfolio.
Inside the studio

How a company actually gets made.

The sequence below is the same for every venture. It is deliberately slow at the front and fast once capital is committed.

Weeks 1–12

Thesis and diligence

A problem surfaces from inside the portfolio or from a client conversation. We size the buyer, name real customers, and pressure-test the wedge against fourteen years of go-to-market experience. Most theses die here, which is the point.

Weeks 6–16

Operator search

Only once the market is validated do we define the ideal operator profile and go looking. We match a business lead and a technical lead with complementary skills, then run the same diligence on them that we run on any investment.

Day 0

Capitalization

The company is incorporated with its own cap table. Capital lands, salaries start, and the founding team is plugged into shared engineering, design, and go-to-market from the first week.

Months 1–6

Proof

Two milestones govern this stage: a working product in front of real customers, and the first signs of repeatable demand. Most ventures reach both within six months.

Months 6–24

Independence

The venture graduates into a company with its own leadership and roadmap. We move to bi-weekly check-ins and bring support where the operator asks for it.

Year 2 onward

Compounding

Profitable and independent, the owners choose: take distributions, position for a sale, or keep compounding. No fund clock forces the answer.

Track record

Fourteen years, seven companies.

The studio did not start with a thesis deck. It started with a company that worked, and then another one.

2011

Double the Donation is founded

A matching gift tool for nonprofits, started without outside investment. It becomes the industry-standard corporate giving and fundraising platform in the US, embedded in tens of thousands of donation flows.

2015

Nexus Marketing scales the sector's content engine

The premier SEO agency connecting the nonprofit sector to investors and for-profit businesses. A decade of ranked sector content becomes the distribution layer the rest of the portfolio launches into.

2020

The portfolio becomes a group

Shared engineering, design, growth, finance, and recruiting are stood up as real functions across the companies rather than as favours between them. This is the beginning of the platform.

2023

Getting Attention is built inside the studio

A Google Ad Grant specialist agency, incubated from a validated thesis and launched with zero outside investment. The first company built end to end on the studio model.

Oct 2024

Tradewing is acquired

A heavily funded association platform with untapped potential. Full rebrand, three new departments, and a rebuilt product cycle follow, with client retention up 28% after the acquisition.

2026

The venture team in the Philippines

The studio stands up a permanent investment and operating bench in the Philippines and opens the Fall 2026 cohort, sourcing 3–5 mission-driven ventures and the leads who will run them.

Where we operate

United States companies. A studio bench in Manila.

Every company in the portfolio serves the U.S. social good economy, and that is where the studio invests. The investment and operating team behind them is being built in the Philippines: seasoned investors and operators working directly with growing US companies in the sector.

Venture leads can be based in the U.S. or the Philippines. What we require is that they are full-time, complementary as a pair, and accountable for a P&L.

Who decides

Decisions are made by people who have run these companies.

There is no investment committee of outsiders. Capital is committed by the partners who operate the businesses funding it: Adam Weinger, who runs Double the Donation, and Rafi Norberg, who runs Nexus Marketing, with Kanwei Li on the technical side.

Around them sits an advisory bench of chief executives and operators from across the nonprofit and association software economy, brought in on diligence, hiring, and go-to-market questions where their judgment is sharper than ours.
